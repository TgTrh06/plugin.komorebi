# Visual language

## Hierarchy and type

- Let spacing, typography, alignment, and composition do most of the hierarchy work before adding surfaces or effects.
- Application headings are clear and proportionate to information density. Landing-page display type may be large when it supports a specific message, not merely to appear premium.
- Keep long-form text comfortably constrained; utility and data content may use wider measures. Use uppercase labels sparingly for short metadata or eyebrows, with deliberate tracking.

## Color and surfaces

- Start with a restrained neutral foundation and a limited, meaningful accent. Choose warm/cool and specific palette from the project brand; lean earthy olive/green only when it fits.
- Apply accent to guide action, orientation, or an intentional brand moment—not indiscriminately to headings, icons, borders, and backgrounds at once.
- Prefer flat sections, subtle borders, and restrained tint. A card earns its surface when it represents a distinct entity, independently actionable unit, or necessary separation.
- Do not nest cards by default. Keep child groups flat or use light background contrast.
- Shadows are restrained structural cues, never a default decorative layer. Use gradients, glow, blur, texture, or glass only for a singular, brand-justified moment with a clear reason.

## Motion

- Product UI motion is short and functional: it explains state, navigation, cause/effect, or feedback.
- Landing and portfolio work may use expressive reveals, hover response, or layered scroll movement when it reinforces the message. Do not repeat an effect everywhere.
- Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, remove or strongly reduce expressive and decorative motion. Retain only brief motion needed to communicate state, interaction, or feedback.
