# Copy and content

Use content to make the interface specific, truthful, and useful. A polished layout does not justify invented product facts.

## Actions and navigation

- Write calls to action around the real action or outcome. Prefer a specific label such as “Create workspace” or “View deployment log” over a generic invitation when the destination is known.
- Keep navigation aligned with destinations that exist. Do not add familiar links merely to complete a header or footer pattern.
- Remove controls that cannot do anything. If an unavailable action is valuable context, label its state honestly and avoid presenting it as ready.

## Claims and evidence

- Do not invent statistics, testimonials, customer identities, security certifications, performance results, integrations, or product capabilities.
- Keep a factual claim only when the project or user supplies support for it. Preserve a source reference when the claim will need later verification.
- When real content is unavailable, omit it or use an unmistakable placeholder such as `[CUSTOMER QUOTE]` or `[VERIFIED METRIC]`. Never disguise placeholder content as final copy.

## Structure and voice

- Let the product narrative determine which sections exist and in what order. Do not fill a standard hero/features/testimonials/FAQ template when the content does not require it.
- Add an FAQ only for product-specific questions grounded in available context. Unknown user concerns are a research gap, not copy to fabricate.
- Replace broad superlatives and fashionable claims with concrete behavior, constraints, or outcomes. Match the project's established vocabulary and the audience's level of knowledge.
- Treat punctuation, sentence length, and tone as contextual voice decisions. Do not ban a typographic convention or common phrase without a product-specific reason.

## Review

Before delivery, confirm that every claim, label, destination, and example is either supported by available information or visibly marked as incomplete. Report material content assumptions with the rest of the delivery assumptions.
