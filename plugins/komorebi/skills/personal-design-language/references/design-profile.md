# Design profile

The preferred character is intentional and warm rather than generic. Working interfaces are quiet, structured, compact enough to be useful, and led by hierarchy, typography, and real information. Marketing and portfolio work may be more editorial, expressive, and cinematic when the brand and message justify it; expression should come from composition, type, imagery, and a purposeful accent, not from a stock visual recipe.

## Decision framework

**Context over templates. Principles over presets. Intentional decisions over generic AI defaults.**

Before introducing a card, nested surface, pill, gradient, large type, grid, motion, illustration, shadow, tinted surface, or icon container, ask what problem it solves. Information hierarchy, interaction semantics, usability, product structure, content narrative, brand identity, and meaningful visual communication are valid reasons. “It looks modern,” an empty page, a common SaaS pattern, or a template convention are not.

When no clear reason exists, prefer typography, spacing, alignment, hierarchy, and content. These rules do not make Komorebi or Itsumori a visual theme for projects.

## Preference spectrum

| Dimension | Default | Contextual shift |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Density | Moderate to compact | Dense for data work; spacious for a meaningful story or campaign. |
| Shape | Subtle to medium rounding | Pills only for tags/badges; reduce rounding on large surfaces. |
| Surfaces | Mostly flat with restrained tint/border separation | Cards only for real entities, actions, or clear separation. |
| Color | Muted, warm, and purposeful | Earthy olive/green is a useful leaning when the brand permits; a designed dark theme is valid. |
| Hierarchy | Clear, restrained in apps | Bold typography and composition are welcome for landing/portfolio work. |
| Motion | Functional in apps | More expressive in landing/portfolio work, never at the expense of control or comfort. |

## Context rules

- Enterprise/admin: favor functional, neutral, compact layouts, scanning, tables, and low visual ceremony.
- Product dashboard: show only action-relevant KPIs; lead with the next decision, then offer detail or raw data.
- Landing/portfolio: a tall, intentional hero is valid when the message earns it. Use real content or imagery and vary composition rather than repeating feature grids.
- Content sites: let audience, subject, and brand decide whether editorial typography is appropriate; do not force it.
- Existing products: preserve recognizable identity and tokens. Improve a pattern only when it harms usability or has a concrete design defect.

## Known preferences

- References are studied for hierarchy, rhythm, typography, imagery, and intentional accents—not copied for palette, grid, components, hero composition, or motifs.
- Photography/editorial imagery is preferred to illustration or 3D when imagery leads. Use imagery in apps sparingly and only when it clarifies or carries brand meaning.
- Serif display type can serve landing-page personality; body text stays highly readable. Monospace is for code or technical metadata, not general prose.
- **Extract WHY before borrowing WHAT.** References such as [ABD](https://abd.works/) and [Huts](https://huts.com/) inform hierarchy, rhythm, typography, imagery, and intentional accent use; they never prescribe a palette, grid, component pattern, hero composition, or motif.

## Decision boundary

These preferences are strong defaults, not a template. A direct request may use a normally avoided treatment after the agent first explains a more suitable alternative; accessibility is never optional.
