# Review checklist

## Story and evidence

- Is the primary action visible in the first reading region?
- Does each dark, red, framed, or stamped treatment have a concrete narrative or interaction reason?
- Are project descriptions, screenshots, technologies, links and claims owner-supplied or clearly qualified?
- Are Japanese phrases supplied/approved and correctly marked with `lang="ja"` where relevant?

## Visual coherence

- Does paper remain the dominant surface, with forest and vermilion used sparingly?
- Does type, spacing and alignment do more work than extra cards, shadows or decoration?
- Is there a meaningful rhythm change between identity, work, approach and contact?
- Are serif/Mincho accents limited to short, purposeful moments?

## Interaction and inclusive use

- Are links and buttons native, keyboard reachable and visibly focused?
- Are primary button press states readable without relying on motion alone?
- Does the language selector expose the current language with `aria-pressed` and preserve page/project context?
- Does email copy retain selectable email text and report success or failure through a persistent inline live status?
- Does mobile menu close with Escape and return focus to its trigger?
- Do reduced-motion settings remove decorative transforms and nonessential animation?

## Responsive and technical checks

- Inspect at 320px, around the navigation breakpoint, and a wide desktop width. Confirm no horizontal overflow.
- Test the longest available English and Japanese strings; permit reflow rather than clipping or shrinking below readable sizes.
- Check text against its actual background; target at least 4.5:1 for normal text.
- Verify image alt text describes real imagery and labels editorial project artwork honestly.
- Verify direct project/case-study URLs, language changes, and page metadata still identify the current project and language.
